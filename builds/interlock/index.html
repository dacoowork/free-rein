Interlock

A zip is two rows of teeth that cannot be pushed together or pulled apart by hand, and one small part that does both. Drag the slider and watch what happens inside it.

Why the teeth hold

Every tooth is a little scoop: a bump on one face and a hollow on the other. Closed, the teeth from the two tapes alternate in one row and each bump sits in the hollow of its neighbour, so the row becomes a chain. Pull the tapes sideways and the bumps only press harder into the hollows.

The parts

  • SliderA hollow metal Y. Its two arms take in the two rows apart; its walls narrow until the rows meet at the throat.
  • DiamondThe wedge between the arms. Going down, it pushes between the rows and tips each tooth out of the next one's hollow.
  • Pin and boxOn a jacket the bottom comes apart. The pin on one tape has to sit in the box on the other, or only one row goes through the slider.
  • StopsLittle clamps at each end that keep the slider on the teeth.
  • ThroatWhere the teeth come out joined. When it spreads with wear, they come out a hair apart and the zip splits behind the slider. Pinching the slider closed with pliers is the old fix.
