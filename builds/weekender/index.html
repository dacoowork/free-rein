pp.2–3 · Welcome

Hello, field.

Welcome to the eleventh Haybale Weekender. Forty-one acts, four stages, one very patient farmer, and an orchard that has been asked to look its best.

One act on the poster has no name. We have told nobody who our Sunday special guest is, not even the stage crew, who have been told only to clear the Main Stage at 21:24 and turn the lights off.

If you'd like to find out early, we've hidden the answer in this programme. Four pages each hold one word: the site map, the Saturday clash-finder, the bar list and the know-before-you-go page. Each one has a box for its answer and a nudge if you get stuck. Find all four words, look at what they have in common, and you'll know who's coming. Your wristband below keeps track.

Clash-finder

Every Saturday set on every stage. Stages are listed left to right as they sit in the field. Tap a set to star it.

My Saturday, by my usual rules. I start with whoever opens the Orchard. Whenever a set finishes, I go straight to whichever set starts next, anywhere on site, because I never walk in halfway through. Four sets, then a long lie down in the tent. Their initials spell what I always come home with.

Jess, a regular, on the Haybale forum

(4)
The Orchard opens at 12:00. That set ends at 13:00: which set anywhere starts soonest after that? Keep going until you have four.

The Moss family's tab

Seven rounds on Saturday, each paid with a fresh strip. On each strip, the last token they tore off was the one they kept as a souvenir. Together the souvenirs spell what the family kept leaving on the tables.

Back cover · Sunday 13 June, 21:24

Who closes Sunday?

Our special guest has been late every year since 2016, and some years hasn't turned up at all. Write their name on your wristband.

(3)
Your four answers each make a longer word, all with the same three letters in front. They're all things you need (or get) when this guest finally shows up.

The sun. Sunburn, sunscreen, sunglasses, sunhat. After two days of grey, the forecast says the cloud breaks on Sunday evening, and sunset in Long Meadow is at 21:24. The Main Stage goes quiet, the lights go off, and four thousand people in wellies sit down in the grass and watch the special guest go down behind the hill. Then Tin Roof Orchestra come back on for one more song.
