Changes, not tunes

Tower bells swing a full circle and can't be hurried, so ringers don't play melodies. They strike in rows, and each change lets a bell move by at most one place. Rounds is the descending scale, 123456.

Handstroke and back

Each bell sounds twice per full pull: once on the sally, once on the tail end. A ringer leaves a beat of silence before every handstroke row, the handstroke gap, which gives ringing its limp.

Place notation

A method is written as the places kept still. x swaps every pair; 16 holds first and sixth and swaps the rest. Six bells have 720 possible rows, and an extent rings them all.

The blue line

Ringers learn a method by the path one bell traces through the rows, drawn in blue in the old method books. Follow it to see dodges, places and the treble hunting straight through.
