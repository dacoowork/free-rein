Sprouts

A pen-and-paper game for two people sharing one screen, invented by John Conway and Michael Paterson at Cambridge in 1967.

How to play

  • Draw a line from a spot to another spot, or loop back to the same one.
  • A new spot sprouts in the middle of your line.
  • Lines can't cross each other or pass through a spot.
  • No spot can have more than three lines. Full spots turn solid.
  • Whoever can't make a move loses.

Each spot starts with three lives. A move spends two and the new spot brings one, so a game with n spots can't last more than 3n − 1 moves.
