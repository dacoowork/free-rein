Waggle

Angle to the sun, and time spent waggling. That is the whole message.

Decoded by Karl von Frisch in the 1940s · Nobel Prize 1973

0:00 / 1:04

    Up means the sun

    Inside the hive it is dark and the comb hangs vertically, so the dancer uses gravity as a stand-in: straight up the comb stands for the sun's direction outside. A run tilted 40° right of up means "fly 40° right of the sun".

    Time means distance

    The longer the waggle run, the farther the flowers: very roughly a second of waggling per kilometre, though the exact rate varies between colonies. Bees seem to judge distance by how much scenery streams past their eyes on the way.

    The dance keeps time

    The sun crosses the sky about 15° an hour. A bee dancing for a long while slowly turns her waggle run to keep pointing at the same patch, adjusting with an internal clock even though she can't see the sky.

    Close to home

    For flowers within a few tens of metres a forager does a looser "round dance" with little direction in it. The waggle dance takes over as distance grows. Followers also smell the flowers on her body.
