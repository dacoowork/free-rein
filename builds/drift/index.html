Drift

Latin turned into Spanish by a handful of sound changes, applied in some order over a thousand years. Take each Latin word and push it through the laws until it comes out as the Spanish one. The order matters.

    The laws

    ĕ and ŏ are short stressed vowels, the ones that broke into two sounds in Spanish. A law that finds nothing to change leaves the word alone.

      Simplified: real sound change happened gradually and in a particular order, and these are the main laws only. The Spanish forms are the standard ones; the step-by-step paths are a teaching model, not a reconstruction of any one word's history.
